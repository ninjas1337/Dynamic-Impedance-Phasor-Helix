Bode and Nyquist are one helix, seen twice

Magnitude, phase and frequency belong on the same three-dimensional curve. Flatten it one way and you get a Bode plot; flatten it the other and you get a Nyquist diagram. These are the three interactive models from the paper, running live: drag the helices to rotate, move the sliders to re-solve the system.

Load frequency control, single-area model. Frequency deviation against its own rate of change, wound along time. The green rings are the decay envelope of a conventional H = 6 s machine; drag inertia down toward H = 1 s and watch the helix punch through them — that gap is the margin lost to declining system inertia.

Series RLC step response. The same second-order geometry in circuit terms: capacitor voltage against its rate of change. L stands in for inertia, R for damping, 1/C for droop. Take R toward zero and the helix rings for many turns; raise it and the spiral collapses into a straight descent.
